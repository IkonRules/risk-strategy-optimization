# Validation Evidence

This document is the evidence ledger for the public portfolio. It states what
can be rerun from a clean checkout, what is supported by retained historical
reports, and how the reported numbers should be interpreted. The model
concepts are explained in [Modelling Approach](MODELLING_APPROACH.md); module
boundaries are documented in [Architecture and status](architecture.md).

## Evidence classes

| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Publicly reproduced** | Generated by the commands and code included in this repository. |
| **Retained historical evidence** | Read from compact reports copied unchanged from the research archive and recorded in the build manifest. These runs are not regenerated by the compact public subset. |
| **Historical reconstruction** | Preserved in the development histories from saved code, artifacts, and earlier records; uncertainties remain explicitly labelled. |
| **Proposed inference** | An architectural conclusion supported by the evidence but not yet implemented or validated as a complete system. |

Results from different classes are not merged into a single benchmark claim.

## Publicly reproduced checks

Run:

```bash
python validation/run_public_validation.py
python -m unittest discover -s tests -v
```

The public checks cover:

- one-versus-one combat-transition mass and selected reference probabilities;
- exact solution of the three-node graph;
- normalization of the four-state terminal distribution;
- deterministic canonical policy selection;
- preservation of two exact tied opening actions;
- a non-zero distribution difference between those labelled tied policies;
- role-preserving graph canonicalization and distribution equivalence;
- analytical total-variation cases; and
- policy-DAG structural and distribution invariants.

The source-extraction checks additionally cover:

- safe imports across all extracted package layers without loading models or
  policy libraries;
- the research-copy combat kernel and exact solver;
- stable role-preserving canonicalization;
- a tiny temporary exact policy-library build and lookup; and
- terminal utility on a compatible synthetic full-board `GlobalState`.

These are lightweight packaging/smoke checks. They do not train ML models, run
Stage A generation, execute expensive Monte Carlo validation, or claim that the
original game engine and experimental full-board routes are end-to-end validated.

The deterministic exact demo result is:

| Quantity | Result |
|---|---:|
| Objective value | `(1.496647620, 2.878787620, 0.580098240)` |
| Canonical root action | `(0, 1)` |
| Exact tied root actions | `2` |
| Evaluated compact states | `15` |
| Terminal support | `4` |
| Normalized probability mass | `1.0` |
| TV between tied labelled-policy distributions | `0.718591140` |

The large tied-policy TV here is caused by labelled symmetry: each optimal
opening first conquers a different defender node. It demonstrates why labelled
successor distributions remain policy-aware. It is not the source of the
separate `0.185074` maximum in the historical branching study.

## Retained historical evidence

The files under [`validation/historical/`](../validation/historical/) are
compact, author-generated reports retained as historical evidence. They were
reviewed for consistency during preparation of the public repository but are
not re-executed by this repository.

| Finding | Conditions | Saved result | Source |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Exact tractability pilot | 360 deterministic cases; 6–8 nodes; troop caps 3–5; 5-second / 100,000-state budget | 360/360 complete; worst `0.783527 s`; max 15,518 states; max support 1,617 | `regional_compounding_validation_v1.md` |
| Exact composition versus MC | One selected regional candidate; 10,000 nested samples | `0.000496 s` exact versus `4.008617 s` MC; MC TV `0.003499` from exact composition | same |

The main conclusion is not that exact solving is universally cheap. It is that
the practical frontier was substantially wider than the earlier loose bounds,
and that combining fixed regional distributions was not the dominant cost.

### Regional approximation and coupling

| Finding | Conditions | Saved result | Source |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Full 50-case benchmark | Regional route versus full exact | mean TV `0.257805`; median about `8e-9`; p90 `1`; 31/50 at TV ≤ `0.05`; seven cases at TV `1` | `regional_compounding_validation_v1.md` |
| Bridge cases | 9 cases | mean TV `0.006117` | same |
| Double-front cases | 10 cases | mean TV `0.797696` | same |
| One versus two regions | Same benchmark | mean TV `0.084156` versus `0.518278` | same |
| Exact candidate selection | 50 cases | candidate changed in 15/50; materially changed in 11/50; 29/50 had exact ties; max seven ties | `regional_compounding_validation_v2.md` |

| Coupled macro-regions | 8 severe cases | macro/full TV `0`; better than independent in 8/8 | `double_front_macro_region_report.md` |

The macro-region result has a strict limitation: the selected macro-region was
the entire graph in all eight saved cases. It verifies that preserving coupling
repairs those examples. It does not validate an algorithm for finding a smaller
sufficient region.

The regional results are intentionally read as a bimodal structural finding.
Weakly coupled bridge cases can be represented extremely well. Double-front,
sequence-opening, outcome-dependent stopping, and front-switching cases can be
severely wrong even when every local region is solved exactly.

|---|---|---:|---|

Export depth changes which alternative tied policies are exposed. It does not
change how deeply the canonical solution is optimized. The experiment also
adds no broad near-optimal tolerance; ties use the existing numerical value
equivalence.

## Historical machine-learning evidence

### Macro statistical phase

An early two-ratio linear model recorded approximately `R² = 0.952`, with
coefficients near `0.344` for troop ratio and `0.823` for territory ratio. The
exact response definition is not securely preserved. Later GLM and GAM
experiments improved response geometry and residual shape but did not solve the
many-to-one mapping from aggregate descriptors to a legal successor board.

This is historical reconstruction, not a reproduced public benchmark.

### Node-level Random Forest phase

Six saved continent model summaries recorded capture ROC-AUC values from
`0.985` to `0.995`, with conditional troop RMSEs. The evaluation used a random
row split rather than an explicitly grouped split by originating state or
scenario. Related rows may therefore appear on both sides of the split. The
metrics are retained as an intermediate milestone, not production-grade
generalization evidence.

### Joint successor-state phase

Later Stage A data represented distributions over complete successor-state
signatures. The retained v2 dataset was explicitly marked provisional and
ineligible for Stage B training because candidate and target sampling were too
sensitive. No final trained transition-distribution bundle or completed
multi-turn Stage E validation is represented in this repository.

## Interpretation rules

### Exactness is conditional

“Exact” refers to exhaustive reachable-state enumeration and probability
propagation under the implemented finite rules and inherited combat kernel. The
kernel itself uses rounded three-decimal transition probabilities. The solver
does not claim exact optimal play for the full commercial game.

### Exact composition does not validate independence

An exact Cartesian product removes sampling error given the regional inputs. It
does not repair a partition that discarded cross-region dependence. Candidate
selection quality, composition quality, and decomposition validity are separate
questions.

### Total variation is not utility

For discrete distributions,

\[
\operatorname{TV}(P,Q)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_x |P(x)-Q(x)|.
\]

TV is a rigorous measure of distributional difference, but it is not a complete
strategic score. It should be read alongside utility, marginals, event
probabilities, runtime, and structural diagnostics.

### Tied value does not imply tied transition

Lexicographic equality says two policies are equivalent under the chosen local
objective. It does not say their labelled successor states or next-wave value
are interchangeable. A downstream model must define a tie convention or retain
policy identity.

### Saved reports are not fresh reproductions

Historical report values remain valuable evidence, but they depend on the code,
hardware, inputs, and budgets of the original runs. The public portfolio keeps
those results separate from clean-checkout validation.

## Evidence-to-architecture conclusions

The evidence directly supports four changes in emphasis:

1. **Try full exact first** within an empirical resource budget.
2. **Preserve sequence coupling** in a full graph or exact macro-region before
   considering independent composition.
3. **Use exact composition** when regional inputs are fixed and the product
   support is manageable.
4. **Treat policy ties explicitly** when successor distributions are training
   targets or inputs to a later decision wave.

It does not yet establish:

- a hardware-independent tractability theorem;
- a general weak-coupling criterion;
- a general smallest-sufficient macro-region algorithm;
- a final tie-handling convention for ML labels; or
- a validated end-to-end exact-first, multi-turn system.
